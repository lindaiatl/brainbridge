Understanding Alzheimer's Disease

What actually happens in the brain — and what it means, day to day, for the people living with it and those who love them.

The BrainBridge Team 8 min read · Reviewed by a neuroscientist
Hero photo

Alzheimer's disease is the most common cause of dementia, yet much of what we hear about it arrives wrapped in fear or jargon. This guide starts from the ground up: what the disease is, how it changes the brain over time, and — just as importantly — what a diagnosis does and does not mean for daily life.

What happens in the brain

Over years, proteins build up and disrupt the connections between neurons, first in the regions that form new memories and later across areas that shape language, judgment, and mood. The pace and pattern differ from person to person — which is why no two journeys look quite the same.

"A diagnosis names what is happening. It does not erase the person, their history, or the moments still to come."

What it means, day to day

Routine, familiarity, and calm surroundings help enormously. So does understanding — for the person living with the disease, and for the family walking beside them. In the resources below, we cover practical steps for caregivers and the science of brain health as we age.
