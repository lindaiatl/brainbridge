In My Dream, Mom Took Me to Work

A childhood memory resurfaces in a dream — and a daughter wakes in tears, wishing she could carry her mother the way she was once carried.

Lindai A personal story · 1 min read

A friend from my hometown recently went back there for a visit. Last night, I asked if she had seen my family yet. She said not yet, but that they had made plans to meet over the weekend.

I told her how much I missed my family.

She gave me several hugs.

That night, I had a dream.

I dreamed of Mom.

In the dream, I had gone home for lunch. Afterward, Mom rode her bicycle to take me back to work.

I sat on the back of her bicycle, lifting my feet up and letting them down again, carefree and happy.

Then suddenly, it struck me: Mom was already so old. How could I let her carry me on her bicycle?

I jumped off, threw my arms around her, and began to cry.

“Mom, I can go to work by myself. You don’t have to take me.”

Mom looked at me as if to say, “Look at you. Look at you. What’s all this about?”

I woke up crying.

In the morning, I sat at the breakfast table, tears silently rolling down my face.

My husband handed me a tissue.

It is very cold today.

I asked my husband, “When do we send winter clothes?”

“On the Cold Clothes Festival,” he said.

I looked it up.

The Cold Clothes Festival is on October 25.

Six more days.

Note: The Cold Clothes Festival (Hanyi Festival) is a traditional Chinese day of remembrance when families honor deceased loved ones, traditionally by sending symbolic winter clothing to keep them warm as the cold season approaches.
